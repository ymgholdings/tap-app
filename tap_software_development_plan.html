01 · Approved product scope

What tap. must do

Visible social discovery

New adult user explicitly opts into ongoing discoverability. See Me stays on until user changes it, with Ghost as optional invisibility. No arbitrary app-side discoverability expiration.

Actual proximity

Show discoverable users within radio range, including users likely in the same public venue. No intentional broad categorization that hides nearby peers. No unsupported promise of precise venue detection.

Personal-data sovereignty

Core operation uses no tap.-owned user database. User profiles, private messages, contact relationships and keys stay on devices; avoid intrusive analytics and unnecessary central telemetry.

Independent adult choices

Nod for friends, Wink for romantic interest, Hush for private chat and Tap for exchange. Permission for presence is not permission to reveal a full profile or send attachments.

Product guardrail: repeated invitations are rate-limited, a decline suppresses further known-peer approaches, and the recipient can choose “No thanks,” “Keep on keepin’ on,” dismiss or block.

03 · User journeys

Interactions and state transitions

Journey A: first-run discoverability

  1. App describes an adult-only nearby network and the implications of visibility.
  2. User declares age; additional age assurance is separately specified if required. Self-declaration must not be described as verified.
  3. User creates a nickname/profile and chooses Nod, Wink, or both.
  4. User explicitly enables See Me. Persist setting locally, without a forced app timer.
  5. App requests OS permissions, explains when background discovery may be degraded, and starts discovery.

Journey B: Wink or Nod request

  1. Discover eligible nearby peer using minimal broadcast information.
  2. Authenticated local exchange reveals only consented profile fields needed to offer an introduction.
  3. Sender makes one request; recipient can accept, politely decline, choose “Keep on keepin’ on,” dismiss or block.
  4. Reject/dismiss updates local invitation state. Rejected known peers cannot repeatedly approach until recipient chooses to reset the boundary.
  5. Mutual acceptance permits Hush or a Tap exchange.

Journey C: physical Tap

  1. Choose contact card, nickname, photos or file payload.
  2. Enter a local transfer session; proximity/accelerometer signal proposes a candidate.
  3. Display peer nickname/key-verification cue and data summary.
  4. Both users approve; authenticated encrypted transfer proceeds over best available local transport.
  5. Show delivered/failed result and a local receipt with no central log.

Journey D: leave wireless range

Hush shows the peer as unavailable. It may retain an encrypted local draft, but must not display a remote-delivery success status. Future remote delivery requires a separately approved architecture decision.

04A · People and intention taxonomy

Discovery filters, activities and connection intentions

The user may configure two separate rules: Who I want to see (outbound discovery filtering) and Who can see or invite me (inbound eligibility). They must not be silently treated as the same permission. Existing contacts can bypass demographic and interest discovery filters when enabled, but never a block. Mutual consent is required before private exchanges.

Who I want to meet

  • Age: inclusive minimum–maximum range, starting at 18
  • Gender: multiple selections, Any, self-description
  • Race / ethnicity: optional, multi-select / self-described
  • Political leaning: Red, Blue, Independent/Other, Any; self-reported
  • Shared interests: selectable categories, keyword and custom tags
  • Connection pathway: Nod, Wink, or both
  • Existing contacts: optionally exempt from filters; blocked peers excluded

What I'm looking for

  • Social: chit-chat, coffee, lunch, dinner, beer, just friends
  • Travel: travel buddy, travel layover buddy
  • Activity: gym, run, walk, hike, bike buddy
  • Learning: tutor me in [subject], language exchange [languages]
  • Dating/intent: dating, DTF, FWB, NSA, looking for a third, open to being a third
  • Orientation and partner interests: Straight, Gay, Bi, plus self-described and multi-select support; not conflated with activity tags
  • Custom activity/intent tags and expiration only when users choose it

Data and match semantics

FieldData modelMeaning
discovery_filtersAge range; gender[], race_ethnicity[], political_leaning[], interest_tags[]; contacts_bypass; inbound_rulesUser-controlled outbound and inbound rules, never inferred from appearance or behavior
activity_tagsArray of controlled vocabulary IDs plus custom tagsMultiple concurrent activity interests; OR/AND selection per category
relationship_intentsArray of intent IDsFriendship, dating, consensual adult arrangements; optional and context-appropriate
orientation_and_partner_preferencesSelf-described orientation + desired partner genders / presentationSeparate from intent and activity, shared only according to explicit disclosure rules
learning_requestssubject, language_pair, seeking_or_offeringStructured tutoring and language exchange matching

Matching priority: block > recognized existing contact (when bypass is enabled) > selected age/gender/race/political/intent criteria > activity interests > eligible nearby candidate. Contact bypass affects which contacts appear to the current user; it must not bypass another person's consent to reveal a profile. Ensure sensitivity-aware disclosure after authenticated connection, not plaintext broadcast.

Adult autonomy: selected preferences are locally entered and locally evaluated; avoid inferring political leanings, race, sexual orientation or age from device signals, photographs, or encounter patterns. No arbitrary app-imposed visibility timeout. The operating systems may still constrain radio operation.

05 · Protocol specification

Implementation contracts (draft)

Wire formats below are semantic examples, not BLE advertisement bytes. They intentionally exclude low-entropy demographic hashes and sensitive profile attributes from public advertising.

{
  "protocol_version": "0.1-draft",
  "discovery": {
    "ephemeral_id": "random_unlinkable_identifier",
    "service_marker": "tap_service",
    "capability_hint": "minimal_supported_transport_bits"
  },
  "invitation": {
    "type": "NOD | WINK",
    "request_id": "random_unique_id",
    "sender_key_binding": "authenticated_session_only",
    "recipient_decision": "PENDING | ACCEPT | DECLINE | DISMISS | BLOCK"
  },
  "exchange": {
    "type": "TAP",
    "recipient_confirmation": "REQUIRED",
    "payload_manifest": ["contact_card", "photo"],
    "application_encryption": "REQUIRED"
  }
}

Proposed transport interface

abstract interface PeerTransport {
  discover(options) -> AsyncStream<PeerCandidate>;
  connect(candidate, timeout) -> SecureChannel;
  send(channel, encryptedFrame) -> TransferResult;
  receive(channel) -> AsyncStream<EncryptedFrame>;
  disconnect(channel, reason) -> void;
  capabilities() -> TransportCapabilities;
}

Open design question: how to deliver private mutual preference matching without revealing sensitive attributes to every discovered peer. Do not implement ad hoc salted-hash equality and call it zero-knowledge.

Nonfunctional targets (provisional)

Privacy

No tap.-hosted personal profile directory; no central messages; no on-device plaintext sensitive fields in discovery broadcast; verified locally enforced choices.

Security

Threat-model review, third-party penetration testing, key lifecycle tests, secure build pipeline, dependency and license audits.

Usability

Full VoiceOver/TalkBack support; clear nearby and unavailable states; explicit payload confirmation; no artificial time-to-discover countdown.

Performance

Define measurable thresholds only after Stage 0 radio characterization: discovery latency distribution, connection success, battery impact, transfer throughput, heat and crash rates.

08 · Dashboard

Who gets a dashboard?

Consumer: the user gets a compact private control center inside the mobile app for visibility, nearby connections, blocked peers, selected sharing data and encrypted conversation settings. No remote administrative dashboard reads those values.

Merchant: a locally hosted tablet or computer interface for signed offer creation, beacon status, offline redemption and merchant-only aggregates. This is shown in the mockup tab above. It is not an operator-level user-monitoring interface.

11 · Delivery sequencing

Milestones and stop/go decisions

Gate 0 · Hardware feasibility 2–4 wk

Native radio testbed and interoperability matrix. Stop or adapt product promises if offline iOS↔Android discovery is not workable.

Gate 1 · Secure protocol 3–5 wk

Security design review, local identity, consent state machines and authenticated transport.

Gate 2 · MVP 4–6 wk

Nearbys screen, opt-in discoverability, Ghost switch, Tap exchange, Nod and basic Hush.

Gate 3 · Wink + controls 3–5 wk

Adult mutual-interest profile, polite/humorous declines, repeated-invite controls and user testing.

Gate 4 · Beta and verification 4–6 wk

Security/UX assessment, radio battery measurements, accessibility and store policy decision.

Gate 5 · Merchant extension later

Separate opt-in merchant product after the social client is stable.

Scheduling note: estimates are planning ranges, not guaranteed sequential durations. No full build commitment before Gate 0 evidence.
